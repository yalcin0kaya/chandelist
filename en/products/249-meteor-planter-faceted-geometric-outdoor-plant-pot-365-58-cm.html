The fracture faces of a stone that fell from the sky. The Meteor Planter comes out of one idea: no face sits at the same angle as its neighbour. The body reaches its widest point at the middle, narrows upward to the mouth and downward to the base — a rock mass standing on end.

FORM Triangular faces wrap the body in three bands. Light travels across them through the day: in the morning one group of facets lifts while its neighbour stays in shadow, at midday the body flattens, in the evening the fracture lines sharpen and the planter becomes another object altogether. A still body that changes all day comes from this.

The smallest of the four. On the floor it comes to about knee height; it can stand alone in an entrance hall, a balcony corner or a wide stair landing. The proportion suits a boxwood ball, a small olive, cacti and groups of succulents.

MATERIAL AND MAKING The body is made from 1.5 mm sheet. Every triangular face is cut, folded and welded to its neighbour; the result is not an assembly of parts but one closed shell. Weld lines are ground back by hand until only the facet lines read from outside. The matt black and matt white bodies are aluminium; the corten-look body is made from steel sheet.

FINISH Matt black and matt white are electrostatic powder coated and oven cured. Because the powder bonds electrostatically and hardens in the oven, it is far tougher than ordinary paint: it does not scratch easily, fade in the sun, blister in damp or flake in winter. The corten look reproduces the mottled grain of oxidised steel — every body carries its own pattern and no two are identical.

WEIGHT AND HANDLING Aluminium does not rust and is far lighter than concrete, stone or terracotta at the same size; it can be moved even when planted. That makes it easy on balconies, terraces and roof gardens with limited load capacity. It does not need to come indoors for winter — it will not crack in frost the way terracotta does.

DRAINAGE AND INDOOR USE There is a drainage hole in the base, so water does not collect in the body. Indoors we recommend using an inner liner (a bucket or plastic pot) that drops inside rather than planting directly into it; water never reaches the floor and the plant can be swapped without emptying the planter.

OPENING AND PLANTING The top opening measures roughly Ø21.5 cm corner to corner. Because the body widens below the rim, the volume inside is noticeably greater than the opening suggests and a root ball settles easily. It suits single-stem trees and rounded forms: olive, lemon, bay, cypress, myrtle, boxwood, bonsai, cacti and succulents. (The plant is not included.)

COLOURS Matt Black, Matt White and Corten Look. Matt black reduces the form to a single silhouette; the lines between the facets read sharpest here and green looks brighter against it. Matt white belongs to the same family as stone and travertine — it steps back against a pale floor and lets the plant lead. The corten look gives the body an earth-toned, oxidised grain; it settles best in a garden or on natural stone. Matt black and matt white are on the aluminium body, the corten look on the steel body.

WHERE IT WORKS Entrances and doorways, terraces and balconies, roof gardens, garden paths, poolsides, hotel and restaurant entrances, shop windows, showrooms and office lobbies. The four heights together build a stepped composition; one on its own anchors a corner. It works indoors too.

CARE Wipe with a damp cloth. Do not use abrasive cleaners, steel wool or solvents. Powder coating needs no maintenance and should not be polished. It can stay outside through winter; just keep the drainage hole clear.
